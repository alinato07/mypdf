## 1. Primary Skin Lesions — Flat Lesions

### Summary

Primary lesions are the **first lesions** that appear in a disease — they exist de novo, without requiring a pre-existing lesion. Mastering primary lesions is the ABC of dermatology and the foundation of clinical diagnosis.

**Flat lesions (colour change only, not elevated):**

- **Macule**
  - Definition: A **flat, circumscribed change in skin colour** — no elevation, no depression, no atrophy
  - Size: **< 1 cm**
  - Examples: Early Vitiligo, Melasma, café-au-lait spots, freckles
  - Key point: Purely a colour change — no palpable component
- **Patch**
  - Definition: Same as a Macule but **> 1 cm**
  - Examples: Large vitiligo patches, large café-au-lait patches, Pityriasis versicolor (extensive)

> **💡 Clinical Pearl:** In exams you must choose the correct term. A flat hypopigmented area < 1 cm = Macule. The same area > 1 cm = Patch. Never call a flat lesion a Plaque — plaques are elevated.

## 2. Primary Skin Lesions — Elevated Solid Lesions

### Summary

**Elevated solid lesions:**

- **Papule**
  - Definition: **Elevated, circumscribed, solid lesion** with no fluid
  - Size: **< 1 cm**
  - Subtypes:
    - **Flat-topped papule** — e.g., Lichen Planus
    - **Umbilicated papule** — e.g., Molluscum Contagiosum (central dell/dimple)
    - **Verrucous papule** — e.g., Common Warts (HPV)
    - **Dome-shaped papule** — e.g., Molluscum, Dermatofibromas
  - Reference: Defined by Bolognia as < 1 cm (some texts say < 0.5 cm)
- **Plaque**
  - Definition: **Elevated, circumscribed, solid lesion** with flat top
  - Size: **> 1 cm** (by Bolognia definition)
  - Same structure as a Papule but larger
  - Classic example: **Psoriatic plaque** (the hallmark lesion of Psoriasis)
- **Nodule**
  - Definition: Like a Papule/Plaque but extends **deep into the dermis or subcutaneous tissue**
  - Key distinguishing feature: Has a **dermal and/or subcutaneous component** — it is deep
  - Palpable deeper structure distinguishes it from a Papule
  - Examples: Dermatofibroma, Lipoma (subcutaneous), Epidermoid cyst
- **Wheal (Urticarial Lesion)**
  - Definition: **Transient elevation of the skin due to dermal oedema**
  - Key feature: **Lasts < 24 hours** (by definition — if > 24 hours, consider Urticarial Vasculitis)
  - Primary lesion of: **Urticaria**

> **💡 Key Distinction — Papule vs. Nodule:** Both are solid elevated lesions, but a Nodule has depth — it extends into the dermis or subcutaneous fat. A Papule is superficial.

> **💡 Wheal Clinical Pearl:** Urticaria resolves within 24 hours by definition. Any wheal lasting > 24 hours requires investigation for Urticarial Vasculitis or other systemic causes.

## 3. Primary Skin Lesions — Fluid-Filled Lesions

### Summary

**Fluid-filled primary lesions:**

| Lesion | Definition | Size | Key Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Vesicle** | Elevated, circumscribed, **fluid-filled** lesion | **< 1 cm** | Chickenpox (Varicella), Herpes Simplex, Herpes Zoster |
| **Bulla** | Elevated, circumscribed, **fluid-filled** lesion | **> 1 cm** | Bullous Pemphigoid, Pemphigus Vulgaris, Burns |
| **Pustule** | Elevated, circumscribed, **pus-filled** (purulent fluid) | Any size | Acne (secondary), Psoriasis pustulosa (primary), Folliculitis |

**Key Notes on Pustules:**
- A Pustule can be either a **Primary lesion** (e.g., in Neutrophilic diseases, Pustular Psoriasis) or a **Secondary lesion** (when infection complicates another lesion)
- Secondary Pustules: e.g., Herpes Simplex with secondary bacterial infection → Pustules; Acne Papule that becomes infected → Pustule
- **Pustules in Neutrophilic disorders** = Primary lesion

> **💡 Why can't Chickenpox be in the Differential Diagnosis of Guttate Psoriasis?**
> Because Chickenpox primary lesion = **Vesicle**, while Guttate Psoriasis primary lesion = **Papule with scales.** The primary lesion immediately excludes Chickenpox.

## 4. Secondary Skin Lesions

### Summary

Secondary lesions **develop from pre-existing primary lesions** (either as disease progression or from external trauma). Understanding the difference between them is a high-yield exam topic.

| Secondary Lesion | Definition | Key Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Crust** | **Dried serum, blood, or pus** on the skin surface | Result of fluid (serum/pus/blood) that has dried; NOT skin itself |
| **Scale** | **Accumulation of Stratum Corneum** (dead keratinocytes) | Hyperkeratosis + Desquamation; the skin itself flaking off |
| **Fissure** | **Linear cleft** in the skin | Extends into dermis; seen in dry skin, palmoplantar conditions |
| **Excoriation** | Exogenous injury to part or all of the epidermis | Scratch marks — from nails or any sharp object; linear/angular |
| **Erosion** | **Partial (superficial) loss of epidermis** | Does NOT reach dermis; heals without scarring |
| **Ulcer** | **Total loss of epidermis ± dermis** | Reaches dermis; heals WITH scarring; essentially a "deep erosion" |

> **💡 High-Yield Exam Distinction — Crust vs. Scale:**
> - **Crust** = Dried biological fluid (serum, blood, pus) sitting ON the skin surface
> - **Scale** = The skin (Stratum Corneum) itself — dead keratinocytes accumulating
> - The distinction was an actual Fellowship exam question!

> **💡 High-Yield Exam Distinction — Erosion vs. Ulcer:**
> - **Erosion** = Superficial (epidermis only) → heals without scar
> - **Ulcer** = Deep (epidermis + dermis) → heals with scar
> - An Ulcer is essentially a **deep erosion**

## 5. Psoriasis — Definition, Epidemiology & Systemic Nature

### Summary

#### Definition
Psoriasis is a **chronic immune-mediated inflammatory disorder** resulting from genetic predisposition combined with environmental triggers, characterised clinically by **sharply demarcated erythematous scaly plaques.**

- Full definition (Bolognia): *"Chronic immune-mediated disorder that results from genetic predisposition combined with environmental triggers, characterised by sharply demarcated erythematous scaly plaques"*
- Core problem: **Abnormal proliferation AND differentiation of the epidermis** — accelerated turnover from 28 days → ~1 week
- Silvery-white scales = accumulation of Stratum Corneum due to rapid, incomplete differentiation
- Erythema = vascular angiogenesis and dilation

#### Epidemiology
- Prevalence: **2% of the world population** (significant — same as Vitiligo and Alopecia Areata)
- **Bimodal age of onset:**
  - **Early onset:** 20–30 years (associated with HLA-Cw6; more severe and familial)
  - **Late onset:** 50–60 years (less genetic association)
- **Equal in both sexes** — no sex predilection
- Psoriasis is a **Systemic Disease**, not merely a skin disease:
  - **Psoriatic Arthritis** (inflammatory joint disease)
  - **Metabolic Syndrome** (Obesity, Hyperlipidaemia, Diabetes, Cardiovascular disease)
  - **Atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease**
  - Patients with Psoriasis have increased risk of all metabolic comorbidities

> **💡 Clinical Pearl:** Always screen Psoriasis patients for Metabolic Syndrome. Ask about smoking, alcohol, obesity. Always review medications. Psoriasis is not just a skin disease — it is a multisystem inflammatory condition.

## 6. Psoriasis — Etiopathogenesis: The Master Triangle

### Summary

The etiopathogenesis of most dermatological diseases — including Psoriasis — follows a **three-factor pathway** that must never be forgotten:

```
Environmental Trigger
        +
Genetically Susceptible Patient
        ↓
  Immune Dysregulation
        ↓
     PSORIASIS
```

This triangle applies to the majority of inflammatory skin diseases.

#### 1. Environmental Triggers — "TIEPOD-LIBA" Mnemonic

| Letter | Trigger | Clinical Significance |
|---|---|---|
| **T** | **Trauma** | Koebner phenomenon — new lesions appear at sites of injury |
| **I** | **Infection** | Streptococcal URTI → Guttate Psoriasis; HIV → severe Psoriasis |
| **E** | **Endocrine** | Hypocalcaemia → trigger for Generalized Pustular Psoriasis |
| **P** | **Psychological Stress** | Worsens existing disease; stress is a major flare factor |
| **O** | **Others** | Alcohol, Smoking, Obesity (all worsen disease) |
| **D** | **Drugs** | See LIBA below |
| **L** | **Lithium** | Psychiatric drug — classic psoriasis trigger |
| **I** | **Interferon** | Biologic/immune modulator — can trigger/worsen psoriasis |
| **B** | **Beta-blockers** | Antihypertensives — classic trigger |
| **A** | **Antimalarials** | e.g., Chloroquine, Hydroxychloroquine — documented trigger |

> **💡 Critical Clinical Pearl:** **Systemic corticosteroids are CONTRAINDICATED in Psoriasis** (except impetigo herpetiformis). Although they produce a dramatic short-term response, sudden withdrawal triggers **Rebound Psoriasis** — often converting stable plaque psoriasis to **Pustular or Erythrodermic Psoriasis.** This is a potentially life-threatening complication.

#### The Koebner Phenomenon
- **Definition:** Formation of psoriasiform lesions in **previously uninvolved skin** of a psoriatic patient following skin injury
- The new lesion appears exactly at the site of trauma
- **Diseases showing true Koebner:** Psoriasis, Vitiligo, Lichen Planus
- **Pseudo-Koebner:** Warts (HPV) — the virus physically transfers to the injured site; not a true immune reaction
- **Reverse Koebner:** Trauma at a psoriatic lesion site may cause the lesion to heal (basis of some Vitiligo treatments)

#### 2. Genetic Predisposition

Evidence for genetic basis:
- **Positive family history in 35–90% of cases**
- Risk with Both parents affected: **41%**
- Risk with One parent affected: **14%**
- Risk with One sibling affected: **6%**
- **Monozygotic twins** show 2–3 fold higher concordance than Dizygotic twins

**HLA Associations (must memorise):**

| HLA | Association | Clinical Relevance |
|---|---|---|
| **HLA-Cw6** | **90% of Early-onset Psoriasis; 50% of Late-onset** | Most important HLA association in Psoriasis |
| **HLA-B27** | **Pustular Psoriasis + Psoriatic Arthritis** | Must be memorised |

## 7. Psoriasis — Immunopathogenesis (Immune Dysregulation)

### Summary

Psoriasis is a **T-cell mediated inflammatory disease.** The specific T-helper subtypes involved are:

### The Three Key T-Helper Subtypes in Psoriasis

| T-Helper Subtype | Key Cytokines Produced | Clinical/Pathological Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Th1** | **TNF-α (Tumour Necrosis Factor alpha)** + IFN-γ | **Angiogenesis** (VEGF ↑) + Keratinocyte **Proliferation** |
| **Th17** | **IL-17A, IL-17F** | **Neutrophil chemotaxis** → Munro microabscesses → Pustular Psoriasis |
| **Th22** | **IL-22** | **Keratinocyte proliferation** → Epidermal Plaque formation |

### The Complete Immunopathogenesis Pathway

```
Environmental Trigger (Infection/Trauma/Drug etc.)
         ↓
Antigen-Presenting Cell (Dendritic Cell) in skin
         ↓  presents via MHC-II
Naive CD4+ T-cell in Lymph Node
         ↓  activates to:
    Th1 → TNF-α → VEGF → Angiogenesis + Adhesion molecules
    Th17 → IL-17 → Neutrophil chemotaxis (Munro/Kogoj microabscesses)
    Th22 → IL-22 → Keratinocyte proliferation (Plaque)
         ↓
T-cells skin-home via CLA + E-selectin
         ↓
Keratinocyte hyperproliferation + Angiogenesis
         ↓
CLINICAL: Erythematous plaque + Silvery-white scales + Auspitz sign
```

### Mechanism of Each Clinical Feature

| Clinical Feature | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Erythema (red base)** | Th1 → TNF-α → VEGF → Angiogenesis → Dilated, elongated, tortuous dermal capillaries |
| **Silvery-white scales** | Th1 + Th22 → Keratinocyte proliferation (28 days → 7 days) + Abnormal keratinisation (Keratin 6, 16, 17 instead of 1, 10) |
| **Auspitz Sign** (pinpoint bleeding on scale removal) | Suprapapillary thinning + dilated dermal papillary capillaries → exposed when scale peeled |
| **Pustules** (Pustular Psoriasis) | Th17 → IL-17 → Massive neutrophil recruitment → Kogoj spongiform pustules |

### Abnormal Keratinisation in Psoriasis
Normal epidermis expresses **Keratin 1 and Keratin 10** in the spinous and granular layers. In Psoriasis:
- Normal K1/K10 is **replaced by K6, K16, K17** — hyperproliferative keratins
- This contributes to abnormal barrier function and the thick scaly appearance

### Biologics Arising from the Pathway

| Target | Biologic Drug | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| **TNF-α** | Infliximab, Adalimumab (Humira), Etanercept | First-generation biologics |
| **IL-12/IL-23 (p40 subunit)** | **Ustekinumab (Stelara)** | Targets shared p40 unit of both IL-12 and IL-23 simultaneously |
| **IL-17A** | **Secukinumab (Cosentyx)**, Ixekizumab, Brodalumab | Most effective for skin clearance |
| **IL-23 (p19 subunit)** | Guselkumab (Tremfya), Risankizumab, Tildrakizumab | Newer, highly selective |

> **💡 Why does Ustekinumab target BOTH IL-12 AND IL-23?** Both interleukins share a common structural unit called **p40.** Ustekinumab binds the p40 subunit — blocking both simultaneously. IL-12 drives Th1 (→ TNF-α) and IL-23 drives Th17 (→ IL-17). Blocking p40 suppresses both pathways.

## 8. Psoriasis — Clinical Features & Auspitz Sign

### Summary

#### Classic Clinical Presentation (Psoriasis Vulgaris)
- **Appearance:** Well-defined (sharply demarcated) **erythematous plaques** with **silvery-white scales**
- **Predilection sites:** Extensor surfaces — **Elbows, Knees, Perisacral region (lumbosacral area)**
- **Symptom:** Classically described as non-pruritic, but many patients report **pruritus** (now recognised as a feature, related to Neutrophil infiltration)

#### The Auspitz Sign (Pathognomonic)
- **What it is:** When scales are peeled off, **pinpoint bleeding points** appear
- **Mechanism:** 
  1. Suprapapillary thinning of the epidermis above dermal papillae
  2. Dilated, tortuous, elongated capillaries just beneath
  3. When scale is removed, these capillaries are immediately exposed → bleeding
- **Clinical significance:** Highly specific for Psoriasis — differentiates from other scaly conditions

#### Post-Inflammatory Changes
- Psoriasis heals leaving **Post-Inflammatory Hypopigmentation** (not Hyperpigmentation as sometimes incorrectly stated)
- There is no distinct "Hypopigmented Psoriasis" type — the hypopigmentation is a PIH (Post-Inflammatory Hypopigmentation) sequela
- Psoriasis + Vitiligo can **coexist** (both are immunological diseases) — especially in Down Syndrome patients

#### Differential Diagnosis of Psoriasis Vulgaris (Erythematous Plaques)

| Differential | Distinguishing Feature |
|---|---|
| **Hypertrophic Lichen Planus** | Violaceous hue, Wickham's striae, different distribution |
| **Dermatomyositis** | Gottron's sign (knuckle papules), photodistribution, muscle weakness |
| **Eczema (Atopic/Contact Dermatitis)** | Vesicles, weeping, lichenification, different distribution |
| **Mycosis Fungoides (MF)** | Psoriasiform plaques but photodistributed, atypical cells on biopsy |

> **💡 Clinical Approach Pearl:** When you see an erythematous scaly plaque — always ask about the PRIMARY LESION first. This guides your entire differential. Psoriasis = Plaque. Guttate = Papule. Chickenpox = Vesicle. Never mix these up.

## 9. Psoriasis — Histopathology (High-Yield)

### Summary

Histopathology of Psoriasis follows logically from the Etiopathogenesis. Learn the features **from top to bottom:**

#### Epidermal Changes (Top to Bottom)

| Feature | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Parakeratosis** | Retention of nuclei in Stratum Corneum — due to rapid proliferation; cells reach corneum before completing differentiation (still have nuclei — normally dead/anucleate) |
| **Absence of Stratum Granulosum (Granular Cell Layer)** | Rapid turnover means cells skip granulosum stage entirely — no Keratohyalin granules formed |
| **Regular Acanthosis** ("Test tube" or "Clubbing" appearance) | Elongation of rete ridges into dermis — uniform/regular pattern; tips of rete ridges club together |
| **Suprapapillary Thinning** | The epidermis ABOVE the dermal papillae is thinned — makes capillaries easily accessible → **Auspitz sign** |
| **Exocytosis** | Lymphocytes migrating into the epidermis (abnormal — normally lymphocytes do NOT reside in epidermis) |
| **Munro Microabscesses** | Small collections of Neutrophils in the Stratum Corneum/upper epidermis — driven by IL-17 |
| **Kogoj Spongiform Pustules** | Larger collections of Neutrophils in the spinous layer — clinically visible as **Pustular Psoriasis** |

#### Dermal Changes

| Feature | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Dilated, elongated, tortuous capillaries** | Angiogenesis driven by VEGF from TNF-α; increased number AND length |
| **Oedema at the tip of dermal papillae** | Contributes to the clubbing/test tube shape of rete ridges |
| **Mixed perivascular inflammatory infiltrate** | Lymphocytes + Macrophages + Neutrophils clustered around dilated blood vessels — these cells then migrate upward into the epidermis |

> **🧠 Memory Strategy — Epidermis Top to Bottom:**
> 1. **Para**keratosis → nuclei retained in corneum
> 2. **Absent** granular layer
> 3. **Regular** acanthosis (test tube appearance)
> 4. **Supra**papillary thinning
> 5. **Exo**cytosis of lymphocytes
> 6. **Munro** microabscesses (small Neutrophil collections)
> 7. **(Kogoj** pustules if Pustular Psoriasis)

> **💡 Exocytosis vs. Epidermotropism:**
> - **Exocytosis** = Normal lymphocytes migrating into epidermis (benign inflammatory response — seen in Psoriasis, Eczema)
> - **Epidermotropism** = Malignant lymphocytes taking up residence in epidermis — hallmark of **Mycosis Fungoides (Primary Cutaneous T-Cell Lymphoma)**

### Translated & Tidied Audio Transcript

**Dr. Eman:** Histopathology — let's work through it from the Etiopathogenesis. We have rapid proliferation (TNF-alpha driving Keratinocyte growth). What happens? Cells are made so fast the factory can't finish them properly. They reach the Stratum Corneum still with their nuclei — this is called **Parakeratosis.** Normal Stratum Corneum = dead, anucleate. **Student:** Parakeratosis.

**Dr. Eman:** IL-17 drives Neutrophil chemotaxis → small Neutrophil collections = **Munro microabscesses.** If they grow large and become clinically visible → **Kogoj spongiform pustules** → Pustular Psoriasis.

**Dr. Eman:** In the Dermis: Dilated, elongated, tortuous blood vessels. Oedema at the tips of dermal papillae (gives the clubbing shape). Memorise it from top to bottom.

## 10. Classification of Psoriasis — The 4-7-2-8 Framework

### Summary

Dr. Eman's teaching mnemonic for classifying all types of Psoriasis: **"4-7-2-8"** (like a football formation)

| Number | Category | Types |
|---|---|---|
| **4** | Major Clinical Types | Psoriasis Vulgaris, Guttate, Erythrodermic, Pustular |
| **7** | Site-Specific Types | Nail, Psoriatic Arthritis, Scalp, Palmoplantar, Mucosal, Flexural (Inverse), Penile |
| **2** | Special Considerations | Childhood Psoriasis, Unstable Psoriasis |
| **8** | Rare Morphological Types | Rupioid, Elephantine, Ostraceous, Digital, Interdigital, Verrucous, Lichenoid, Follicular |

---

### The 4 Major Types

#### Type 1: Psoriasis Vulgaris (Most Common — ~90% of cases)
- **Definition:** Well-defined erythematous plaques with silvery-white scales
- **Sites:** Elbows, Knees, Perisacral area
- **Differential:** Hypertrophic Lichen Planus, Dermatomyositis (Gottron sign), Eczema, Mycosis Fungoides

#### Type 2: Guttate Psoriasis
- **Appearance:** Multiple **small papules** (raindrop-like) with **thin scales** scattered widely over the trunk
- **"Guttate"** = Latin for "drops" — describes the small drop-like papules
- **Trigger:** Classically **Streptococcal upper respiratory tract infection** (especially in children)
- **Population:** Mainly **children and young adults**
- **Differential:**
  - **Pityriasis Rosea** (Collarette scales, Herald patch, follows skin lines)
  - **PLC (Pityriasis Lichenoides Chronica)** — Mica scales, different morphology
  - **Secondary Syphilis** (Psoriasiform eruption — must always consider)
  - **NOT Chickenpox** — Chickenpox primary lesion = Vesicle, not Papule

#### Type 3: Erythrodermic Psoriasis
- **Definition:** Generalised erythema AND scaling involving **> 90% body surface area**
- **Severity:** Requires **hospitalisation** — a medical emergency
- **Triggers for conversion to Erythroderma:**
  - **Sudden withdrawal of systemic corticosteroids** (most important)
  - Infection
  - Other drug triggers (Lithium, Beta-blockers, etc.)
- **Top 3 causes of Erythroderma in adults:** Psoriasis, Drugs, Atopic Dermatitis
- **Management:** Hospitalisation, rule out infection, carefully initiate systemic therapy

> **⚠️ Critical Warning:** Systemic corticosteroids are ABSOLUTELY CONTRAINDICATED in Psoriasis. Even though there is a dramatic initial response, abrupt discontinuation triggers rebound flare → often Pustular or Erythrodermic Psoriasis — which may be life-threatening.

#### Type 4: Pustular Psoriasis
Divided into **Generalised** and **Localised:**

**A) Generalised Pustular Psoriasis:**

| Subtype | Key Features |
|---|---|
| **Acute Generalised Pustular Psoriasis (Von Zumbusch)** | Sudden onset; high fever; sheets of sterile pustules on erythematous skin; systemic illness; HLA-B27 associated |
| **Generalised Pustular Psoriasis of Pregnancy (Impetigo Herpetiformis)** | Occurs in pregnancy; high maternal + fetal morbidity; often requires systemic treatment; exception to steroid rule |
| **Circinate / Annular / Linear / Exanthematous** | Various patterns of generalised pustular eruptions |
| **Infantile Pustular Psoriasis** | Occurs in children/infants |

**B) Localised Pustular Psoriasis:**

| Subtype | Key Features |
|---|---|
| **Palmoplantar Pustulosis (PPP)** | Sterile pustules confined to palms and soles; HLA-B27 associated; smoking is a major trigger |
| **Acrodermatitis Continua of Hallopeau** | Pustules on fingers/toes around nails; progressive and destructive |

> **💡 Hypocalcaemia as Trigger:** Hypocalcaemia specifically triggers **Generalised Pustular Psoriasis** — the Endocrine (E) factor in TIEPOD-LIBA.

## 11. MCQ Practice & Clinical Pearls

### Summary

#### High-Yield MCQ Points

- **Koebner Phenomenon:** Formation of psoriasiform lesions in **uninvolved** skin following trauma → Psoriasis, Lichen Planus, Vitiligo
- **Pseudo-Koebner:** Warts (physical viral transfer to wound) — NOT a true Koebner
- **HLA-Cw6:** 90% of Early-onset Psoriasis
- **HLA-B27:** Psoriatic Arthritis + Pustular Psoriasis
- **Munro microabscesses:** Small Neutrophil collections in corneum/upper epidermis (all Psoriasis types)
- **Kogoj pustules:** Large Neutrophil collections → clinically = Pustular Psoriasis
- **Auspitz sign:** Pinpoint bleeding on scale removal → Suprapapillary thinning + dilated capillaries
- **Absence of Stratum Granulosum:** Pathognomonic of Psoriasis (vs. Lichen Planus = thick/hypergranulosis)
- **Parakeratosis:** Retained nuclei in Stratum Corneum
- **Steroid contraindicated in Psoriasis:** Risk of rebound Pustular/Erythrodermic flare
- **Trigger for Generalized Pustular Psoriasis:** Hypocalcaemia (Endocrine factor)
- **Trigger for Guttate Psoriasis:** Streptococcal URTI
- **Erythroderma definition:** > 90% BSA involvement
- **Top 3 causes of Erythroderma:** Psoriasis, Drugs, Atopic Dermatitis
- **Ustekinumab mechanism:** Targets p40 subunit shared by IL-12 AND IL-23
- **Secukinumab / Cosentyx:** Anti-IL-17A
