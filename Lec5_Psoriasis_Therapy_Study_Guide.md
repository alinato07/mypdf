## 1. Overview of Psoriasis Treatment Categories

### Summary

- Psoriasis therapy is divided into **five main categories**:
  1. **Topical therapies** — first-line for mild/localised disease
  2. **Systemic therapies** — for moderate-to-severe disease
  3. **Phototherapy** — UVB (broadband/narrowband), PUVA
  4. **Photodynamic therapy**
  5. **Laser** (Excimer, PDL, CO₂)
- **Special situations** require tailored approaches (pregnancy, comorbidities, childhood, nail psoriasis, psoriatic arthritis)
- **General measures** (emollients, lifestyle, avoiding triggers) are always part of management

## 2. Indications for Systemic Therapy

### Summary

Systemic therapy is indicated when psoriasis is **severe**, **widespread**, or **unresponsive** to topical treatment. Specific indications include:

- **Chronic plaque psoriasis** involving >10–15% body surface area (BSA)
- **Psoriasis not responding** to topical therapies
- **Erythrodermic psoriasis**
- **Pustular psoriasis** (generalised or localised)
- **Moderate-to-severe psoriatic arthropathy**
- **Severe nail psoriasis**
- **Psoriasis of small surface area** but severely impairing quality of life (e.g., severe palmoplantar or scalp psoriasis)

### Translated & Tidied Audio Transcript

## 4. Methotrexate (MTX)

### Summary

#### Mechanism of Action
- MTX is a **folate antagonist** — it inhibits **dihydrofolate reductase (DHFR)**, which is essential for DNA synthesis
- This leads to inhibition of **purine synthesis** and **thymidylate synthesis**, blocking cell proliferation
- MTX polyglutamate (the active intracellular form) also inhibits **MTHFR** (methylenetetrahydrofolate reductase), reducing methionine and SAM production
- Net effect: **anti-proliferative** (stops keratinocyte proliferation), **anti-inflammatory**, and **immunosuppressive**

#### Dose
- **7.5–25 mg** as a **single oral or IM dose once weekly**
- Always given with **folic acid 1–5 mg/day** (every day *except* the day of MTX) to reduce side effects (especially mucositis and bone marrow suppression)

#### Indications
- Severe psoriasis (chronic plaque >10–15% BSA, pustular, erythrodermic)
- Psoriatic arthritis (moderate to severe)
- Psoriasis not responding to topical treatments, phototherapy, or retinoids

#### Side Effects
- **Subjective:** Nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, fatigue, headache
- **Hepatic:** Hepatitis, **fibrosis**, **cirrhosis** (most feared long-term effect)
- **Haematopoietic:** Leucopenia, thrombocytopenia, anaemia
- **Mucocutaneous:** Oral erosions/ulcers, alopecia, delayed phototoxicity, plaque necrosis (overdose)
- **Pulmonary:** Interstitial pneumonitis (acute-onset cough + dyspnoea — idiosyncratic)
- **Teratogenic** (Category X) — discontinue **3 months** before planned conception (both male and female)
- **Carcinogenesis:** Increased risk of lymphoma (especially in RA patients); increased cutaneous SCC risk in PUVA-treated patients
- **Opportunistic infections:** *Pneumocystis jirovecii* pneumonia, cryptococcosis

#### Absolute Contraindications
Pregnancy, severe anaemia/leucopenia/thrombocytopenia, significant liver disease, active infections, hypersensitivity to MTX, active peptic ulcer, significant pulmonary dysfunction, immunodeficiency syndromes

#### Monitoring
- CBC, liver function tests, kidney function tests
- Pregnancy test
- Tests for HIV / PPD (TB screening)
- Chest X-ray
- **Liver biopsy** (if cumulative dose concern or abnormal LFTs)

#### Clinical Pearl
> ⚠️ **Folic acid supplementation** is mandatory with MTX — it does NOT reduce efficacy but significantly reduces side effects. The drug is given **once weekly** (not daily) — a common real-world medication error.

## 5. Acitretin

### Summary

#### What Is It?
- Acitretin is an **oral retinoid** (vitamin A derivative) — a **second-generation retinoid**
- It is the active metabolite of etretinate

#### Mechanism of Action
- Binds to **nuclear retinoic acid receptors (RAR/RXR)**
- **Normalises keratinocyte differentiation** and **reduces proliferation**
- Has anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory properties

#### Dose
- **10–50 mg/day** as a **single daily dose** for **3–6 months**

#### Indications
- Severe psoriasis not manageable by topical or phototherapy
- **Monotherapy** is preferred for **erythrodermic** or **pustular psoriasis**
- **Combination therapy** (with UVB or PUVA — "Re-PUVA") is used for **chronic plaque psoriasis**
- **First-line choice for pustular psoriasis of pregnancy (impetigo herpetiformis)** — *Note: this is a paradox — acitretin itself is teratogenic, so in pregnancy the preferred option is cyclosporine or UVB*

#### Side Effects
- **Hyperlipidaemia** (especially hypertriglyceridaemia) — monitor lipid profile
- **Cheilitis** (lip dryness/cracking) — very common, almost universal
- **Xerosis** (dry skin), dry eyes, dry mouth
- **Hepatotoxicity** — monitor LFTs
- **Teratogenic (Category X)** — must avoid pregnancy during treatment AND for **3 years after stopping** (due to re-esterification back to etretinate which has a very long half-life)
- Diffuse idiopathic skeletal hyperostosis (DISH) with long-term use
- Hair thinning, nail changes

#### Absolute Contraindications
- Pregnancy or lactation
- Women of childbearing potential who cannot guarantee contraception during AND **3 years after** discontinuation
- Severe liver or kidney dysfunction
- Uncontrolled hyperlipidaemia
- Concomitant methotrexate (both are hepatotoxic)

#### Monitoring
- Pregnancy test (before starting)
- Lipid profile (baseline and during treatment)
- CBC, liver and kidney function tests

#### Clinical Pearls
> ⚠️ **The "3-year rule"**: Acitretin is teratogenic for **3 years after stopping** — this is unique among systemic drugs and must be counselled clearly to all women of childbearing age.
> 💊 Acitretin + alcohol = converts back to **etretinate** (extremely long half-life teratogen) — patients must avoid alcohol completely.

### Translated & Tidied Audio Transcript

**Dr. Eman:** Now acitretin. Acitretin is a retinoid — a derivative of Vitamin A. Its mechanism of action is that it normalises differentiation of keratinocytes. In psoriasis, the keratinocytes proliferate abnormally and their differentiation is disrupted. Acitretin corrects this. It is also anti-inflammatory.

**Dr. Eman:** The dose is 10 to 50 milligrams per day, given as a single daily dose for 3 to 6 months. Now, regarding the indications: it is the **drug of choice as monotherapy for erythrodermic psoriasis and pustular psoriasis**. For chronic plaque psoriasis, we combine it with phototherapy.

**Dr. Eman:** The most important side effect to remember is that it is **teratogenic** — Category X. But unlike methotrexate, which we stop 3 months before conception, with acitretin you need to wait **3 years** after stopping the drug before attempting pregnancy. This is because acitretin can be converted back into etretinate in the body — especially if the patient drinks alcohol — and etretinate has a very long half-life and is also teratogenic. So alcohol is strictly forbidden with acitretin.

**Dr. Eman:** Other side effects: cheilitis — almost all patients get dry, cracked lips. Xerosis — dry skin. Hyperlipidaemia — especially high triglycerides — so we monitor the lipid profile. And hepatotoxicity — so liver function tests are monitored. It should **not** be combined with methotrexate because both are hepatotoxic.

**Dr. Eman:** No, absolutely not — it is Category X. But there is a special situation: pustular psoriasis of pregnancy — called impetigo herpetiformis — is managed preferably with **cyclosporine or UVB**, not acitretin.

## 6. Cyclosporine

### Summary

#### Mechanism of Action
- **Calcineurin inhibitor** — binds to **cyclophilin**, forming a complex that inhibits **calcineurin**
- This blocks the activation of **NFAT** (Nuclear Factor of Activated T-cells), suppressing transcription of **IL-2** and other cytokines
- Net effect: profound **T-cell immunosuppression**

#### Dose
- **2.5–5 mg/kg/day** in **divided doses** (given twice daily)
- Maximum duration: **1–2 years** (due to cumulative nephrotoxicity)

#### Indications
- Severe psoriasis not responding to or inappropriate for conventional therapies
- **Short-term rapid control** of severe flares
- Erythrodermic psoriasis, generalised pustular psoriasis
- Psoriasis in patients who need quick response (bridging therapy)

#### Side Effects
- **Nephrotoxicity** — most important; monitor creatinine and BUN regularly
- **Hypertension** — very common; may require antihypertensive treatment
- **Malignancy risk** (especially skin cancers with long-term use)
- **Immunosuppression** — increased infection risk
- **Hypertrichosis** (excess hair growth)
- **Gingival hyperplasia** (gum overgrowth)
- Hyperlipidaemia, hyperuricaemia, hyperkalemia
- Drug interactions: many drugs affect cyclosporine metabolism (CYP3A4)

#### Absolute Contraindications
- Abnormal renal function
- Uncontrolled hypertension
- Active malignancy or history of malignancy
- Active serious infections
- Live vaccinations
- Concurrent methotrexate
- Pregnancy or lactation
- Concurrent immunosuppressive therapy

#### Monitoring
- **Blood pressure** (at every visit)
- **Kidney function tests:** BUN, creatinine, urine analysis
- **Lipid profile**

#### Clinical Pearls
> 🔑 Cyclosporine is the **fastest-acting** first-line systemic agent — useful for acute severe flares
> ⏱️ Maximum use: **1–2 years** — then must switch (due to irreversible nephrotoxicity risk)
> 💊 Cyclosporine + NSAIDs or aminoglycosides = dangerous nephrotoxicity combination

### Translated & Tidied Audio Transcript

**Dr. Eman:** Now cyclosporine. Cyclosporine is a calcineurin inhibitor. It works by binding to a protein called cyclophilin, and this complex inhibits calcineurin, which is needed to activate T-cells. So basically it suppresses the immune system by blocking T-cell activation and IL-2 production.

**Dr. Eman:** The dose is 2.5 to 5 milligrams per kilogram per day, given in divided doses. This is very important — it is weight-based. And we don't use it for more than 1 to 2 years because it is nephrotoxic — meaning it damages the kidneys with long-term use.

**Dr. Eman:** The side effects: the most important one is **nephrotoxicity** — kidney damage. We must monitor kidney function regularly — BUN, creatinine, urine analysis. It also causes **hypertension**, so we monitor blood pressure at every visit. And it causes **gingival hyperplasia** — swelling of the gums — and **hypertrichosis** — excessive hair growth.

**Dr. Eman:** The contraindications: if the kidney function is impaired, we cannot use it. If the blood pressure is not controlled, we cannot use it — unless we can bring it under control, but we must be careful. It is also contraindicated with active malignancy.

**Student:** Doctor, can it be used in pregnancy?

**Dr. Eman:** In general it is contraindicated in pregnancy. However, in certain special situations — like pustular psoriasis of pregnancy (impetigo herpetiformis) that is severe — cyclosporine may be used with caution because it is Category C, while acitretin is Category X. It is the lesser of two evils in that situation. But it should be used with careful monitoring.

## 7. Biologic Therapies (Targeted Therapy)

### Summary

#### What Are Biologics?
- **Protein molecules derived from living organisms** that target specific proteins in the immune system
- Three types:
  1. **Monoclonal antibodies (mAb)** — e.g., infliximab, adalimumab, ustekinumab, secukinumab
  2. **Fusion antibody proteins (cept)** — e.g., etanercept
  3. **Recombinant human cytokines**

#### Naming Convention
- **-mab** = monoclonal antibody
- **-cept** = fusion protein (receptor-Fc)
- **-xi-** = chimeric (part mouse, part human)
- **-zu-** = humanised
- **-u-** = fully human

#### Targets and Agents

| Biologic Agent | Target | Molecule Type | FDA/EMA Approval |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Etanercept** | TNF-α (soluble + transmembrane) | Human recombinant receptor-Fc fusion protein | FDA + EMA |
| **Infliximab** | TNF-α | Chimeric monoclonal antibody (IgG1) | FDA + EMA |
| **Adalimumab** | TNF-α | Fully human monoclonal antibody (IgG1) | FDA + EMA |
| **Ustekinumab** | p40 subunit of **IL-12/23** | Human monoclonal antibody | FDA + EMA |
| **Secukinumab** | **IL-17A** | Human monoclonal antibody | FDA + EMA |
| **Ixekizumab** | **IL-17A** | Humanised monoclonal antibody | FDA + EMA |
| **Brodalumab** | **IL-17 receptor (IL-17RA)** | Human monoclonal antibody | FDA + EMA |
| **Guselkumab** | **IL-23 (p19 subunit)** | Human monoclonal antibody | FDA |

#### Doses (Key Agents)
| Agent | Brand Name | Target | Dose & Route |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Infliximab** | Remicade | TNF | 5 mg/kg IV infusion at 0, 2, 6 weeks → then every 8 weeks |
| **Adalimumab** | Humira | TNF | 80 mg loading → 40 mg every other week SC |
| **Etanercept** | Enbrel | TNF | 50 mg twice/week for 3 months → then once/week SC |
| **Ustekinumab** | Stelara | IL-12/23 | 45 mg SC at 0 and 4 weeks → then every 12 weeks |
| **Secukinumab** | Cosentyx | IL-17A | 300 mg weekly SC for 4 weeks → then monthly SC |

#### Side Effects of All Biologics
1. **Increased risk of infections** — particularly:
   - **Reactivation of latent TB** (screen before starting!)
   - Bacterial sepsis
   - Systemic fungal infections (e.g., histoplasmosis)
   - **Hepatitis B virus reactivation** (screen before starting!)
2. **Increased risk of malignancies** — especially lymphomas
3. **Hypersensitivity reactions** — urticaria, angioedema, up to **anaphylaxis** (especially infliximab IV)
4. **Cardiovascular and CNS complications**
5. **Injection site reactions** (SC agents)
6. **Candidiasis** (especially IL-17 inhibitors — secukinumab, ixekizumab)
7. **Inflammatory bowel disease exacerbation** — IL-17 inhibitors contraindicated in IBD
8. **Demyelinating disease** — avoid TNF inhibitors in MS

#### Pre-Treatment Screening (Baseline Monitoring for ALL Biologics)
1. **TB screening:** PPD test + Interferon-γ release assay (IGRA) + chest X-ray
2. **Hepatitis B and C** serology — if HBsAg positive, must treat with antiviral (e.g., tenofovir, entecavir) before starting biologic
3. **HIV testing**
4. **CBC**
5. **Kidney and liver function tests**

#### Mechanism Insight (Immunology)
- Psoriasis is driven by: **Dendritic cells → IL-12/IL-23 → Th1/Th17 cells → IL-17, IL-22, TNF-α → keratinocyte proliferation and inflammation**
- TNF inhibitors break the inflammatory cycle broadly
- IL-12/23 inhibitors (ustekinumab) block both Th1 and Th17 pathways
- IL-17 inhibitors are the **most targeted and most effective** for plaque psoriasis

#### Clinical Pearls
> 🔑 **Infliximab** = chimeric → highest anaphylaxis risk; given IV → fastest onset
> 🔑 **Etanercept** = NOT a monoclonal antibody — it is a **fusion protein** (soluble TNF receptor + IgG1 Fc)
> 🔑 **Secukinumab & ixekizumab** (anti-IL-17) → risk of **oral candidiasis** and contraindicated in **Crohn's disease**
> 🔑 **Brodalumab** blocks the **receptor** for IL-17, not IL-17 itself — broadest IL-17 blockade
> ⚠️ Always screen for **latent TB** before starting ANY biologic — treat if positive before initiating therapy

## 8. Phototherapy

### Summary

#### Definition
- **Phototherapy:** Use of **ultraviolet radiation (UVR)** for treatment of skin diseases
- **Photochemotherapy (PUVA):** Combination of a **photosensitiser** (psoralen) with UVR

#### Types of UV Radiation
| Type | Wavelength | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **UVC** | 100–280 nm | Filtered by atmosphere; not used therapeutically |
| **UVB** | 290–320 nm | Most commonly used |
| **UVA** | 320–400 nm | Used with psoralens (PUVA) or as UVA1 (340–400 nm) |

#### Phototherapy Modalities
- **BB-UVB** (Broadband UVB): 290–320 nm
- **NB-UVB** (Narrowband UVB): **311–313 nm** — most commonly used; better efficacy and safety than BB-UVB
- **PUVA** (Psoralen + UVA): systemic or topical psoralens combined with UVA
  - **Systemic PUVA**: oral psoralen (8-methoxypsoralen) + UVA
  - **Topical PUVA**: topical psoralen cream/bath + UVA
- **UVA1** (340–400 nm): used mainly for morphoea, atopic dermatitis
- **Excimer laser** (308 nm): targeted NB-UVB for localised plaques

#### Indications for Phototherapy in Psoriasis
- **Moderate-to-severe psoriasis** not responding to topical therapies
- Can be used as **monotherapy** or **in combination** with topical or systemic agents
- **Re-PUVA** = acitretin + PUVA (synergistic, allows lower doses of both)

#### Mechanism of Action
- **Anti-proliferative:** Inhibits keratinocyte proliferation (DNA damage/apoptosis)
- **Immunomodulatory:**
  - Depletion of **Langerhans cells** (APCs) from the skin
  - Shift in cytokine profile: **Th1 → Th2** (reduces the pro-inflammatory Th1/Th17 response)

#### Side Effects
- **Acute:**
  - **Erythema and burns** (most common)
  - Tanning/hyperpigmentation
  - Reactivation of latent viral infections (e.g., HSV)
  - Photosensitivity
- **Chronic:**
  - **Photoageing** (premature ageing of skin)
  - **Increased risk of cutaneous malignancy** (especially SCC and melanoma with PUVA)
- **Psoralen-specific side effects (PUVA):**
  - Nausea, vomiting
  - Hepatotoxicity
  - **Increased risk of cataract** → patients must wear **UV-protective goggles** during treatment and for 24 hours after taking oral psoralens

#### Precautions
- Wear **protective UV goggles** during treatment
- Use **genital shields** (especially in males) to protect gonads
- Avoid PUVA in patients on cyclosporine (increased SCC risk)

#### Contraindications
- **Photosensitive dermatoses** (e.g., lupus erythematosus, porphyria)
- **Genetic disorders** with increased photosensitivity or skin cancer risk (e.g., xeroderma pigmentosum)
- **Current or previous skin cancer**
- Patients on **cyclosporine** or other immunosuppressants (increased skin cancer risk)
- History of **>200 PUVA treatments** (lifetime limit)

#### Baseline Monitoring
- ANA / anti-Ro (if indicated — to rule out lupus)
- Liver function tests and **eye examination** (specifically for PUVA — cataract risk)
- Cancer screening (if indicated)

#### Clinical Pearls
> 🔑 **NB-UVB (311–313 nm)** is the **preferred phototherapy** for most patients — better than BB-UVB, safe in pregnancy (Category B), first-line in children
> ⚠️ **PUVA** = much higher carcinogenic risk than NB-UVB — cumulative dose tracked (lifetime limit ~200 sessions)
> 👁️ Cataract risk from PUVA — **oral psoralen patients must wear UV goggles for 24 hours** after taking the pill
> 🤰 **NB-UVB is safe in pregnancy** — preferred over systemic agents when possible

## 9. Psoriasis in Special Situations & Comorbidities

### Summary

#### Psoriasis + Liver Disease
- **Avoid:** Methotrexate (hepatotoxic), acitretin (hepatotoxic)
- **Preferred:** Phototherapy **(UVB)**, biologics (etanercept > adalimumab, ustekinumab > secukinumab)

#### Psoriasis + Hepatitis B or C Infection
- Screen all patients before starting systemic therapy
- **HBsAg positive:** Measure HBV DNA → treat with antiviral (tenofovir, entecavir, telbivudine) **before** starting immunosuppressive therapy
- Monitor for reactivation in those with anti-HBs or anti-HBc antibodies (occult infection)
- **Preferred biologics:** Etanercept > adalimumab; ustekinumab > secukinumab

#### Psoriasis + Metabolic Syndrome
- **Avoid:** Cyclosporine (hypertension, nephrotoxicity, hyperlipidaemia)
- **Preferred:** UVB phototherapy, biologics (TNF inhibitors may improve metabolic parameters)

#### Psoriasis + Immunosuppression (e.g., HIV/AIDS)
- **Preferred:** UVB phototherapy, retinoids
- Avoid immunosuppressives in uncontrolled HIV
- Biologics can be used if HIV is well-controlled on antiretrovirals

#### Psoriasis + Pregnancy
- **Preferred:** Topical therapies (mild-to-moderate strength corticosteroids), **NB-UVB**
- **Category C (used with caution):** Cyclosporine
- **Category B:** TNF inhibitors (etanercept, adalimumab), ustekinumab, secukinumab
- **Category X (absolutely avoid):** Methotrexate, acitretin
- **Pustular psoriasis of pregnancy (impetigo herpetiformis):** First-line = **systemic corticosteroids** or cyclosporine
- ⚠️ **Systemic corticosteroids are generally avoided** in psoriasis because of the risk of **rebound severe flare** upon withdrawal — but in impetigo herpetiformis, the benefit outweighs the risk

#### Psoriasis + History of Malignancy (Lymphoma, Lung Cancer)
- **Avoid:** Methotrexate, cyclosporine, TNF inhibitors (may promote tumour growth)
- **Preferred:** UVB, vitamin D analogues, retinoids

#### Psoriasis + History of Frequent Skin Cancers
- **Avoid:** PUVA (increases SCC risk), cyclosporine
- **Preferred:** NB-UVB (if needed), vitamin D analogues, retinoids

#### Childhood Psoriasis
- **Therapeutic challenge** — use topical therapy as much as possible (first-line)
- **NB-UVB** for moderate-to-severe cases
- Systemic therapy (MTX, biologics) for severe cases only

#### Nail Psoriasis
- Treatment depends on **severity**
- Options: topical (corticosteroids, vitamin D analogues, tazarotene), **intralesional injections** (triamcinolone), systemic (MTX, acitretin, biologics), phototherapy
- **Avoid trauma**, use emollients, regular nail trimming

#### Psoriatic Arthritis
- **Mild:** NSAIDs
- **Moderate-to-severe:** **DMARD** — **Methotrexate** is first-line
- Biologics (TNF inhibitors, IL-17 inhibitors) for DMARD-resistant cases
- **Avoid systemic corticosteroids** — risk of post-steroid severe psoriasis flare

#### Pustular Psoriasis (Generalised — GPP)
- **Drug of choice: Acitretin (systemic retinoid)**
- In pregnancy: prednisone / cyclosporine

## 11. High-Yield Exam Points

### Summary

1. **MTX dose:** Once **weekly** (not daily!) — the most common real-world error
2. **MTX + folic acid:** Always given together; folic acid every day EXCEPT the day of MTX
3. **Acitretin teratogenicity:** Contraception required for **3 years** after stopping
4. **Acitretin + alcohol → etretinate** (prolonged teratogen) — alcohol strictly forbidden
5. **Cyclosporine max duration:** 1–2 years (nephrotoxicity)
6. **Biologics pre-screening:** TB (PPD + IGRA + CXR), Hep B/C, HIV, CBC, LFTs, RFTs
7. **IL-17 inhibitors (secukinumab) → candidiasis** and **contraindicated in Crohn's disease**
8. **Infliximab → anaphylaxis risk** (IV chimeric antibody)
9. **NB-UVB (311–313 nm):** Most commonly used phototherapy; safe in pregnancy and children
10. **PUVA → cataract risk:** Wear UV goggles during treatment AND for 24 hours after oral psoralens
11. **Systemic corticosteroids avoided** in psoriasis → risk of rebound flare (exception: impetigo herpetiformis)
12. **Pustular psoriasis drug of choice:** Acitretin
13. **Psoriatic arthritis first-line DMARD:** Methotrexate
14. **Drug of choice in psoriasis + liver disease:** UVB + etanercept (preferred biologic)
15. **HBsAg positive patient:** Must give antiviral therapy BEFORE starting biologic
