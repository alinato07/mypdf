## 2. Generalised Pustular Psoriasis — All Subtypes

### Summary

Pustular psoriasis arises when **Neutrophil infiltration dominates** the histopathology. All forms of psoriasis contain small collections of neutrophils (Munro microabscesses), but when these enlarge to Kogoj spongiform pustules and become clinically visible, we have **Pustular Psoriasis.**

#### Triggering Factors for Pustular Psoriasis (any type)
- **Rapid tapering of systemic corticosteroids** (most important — reason steroids are contraindicated)
- **Pregnancy**
- **Hypocalcaemia**
- **Infection**
- **Topical irritants** (e.g., Coal tar, if applied to inflamed skin)

#### Subtype 1: Acute Generalised Pustular Psoriasis — Von Zumbusch

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Presentation** | **Emergency case** — systemic illness with burning skin pain, fever, malaise |
| **Appearance** | Sheets of erythema **studded with small sterile pustules** (so densely packed pustules look like crusting) |
| **Distribution** | Starts at **flexures**, spreads to previously unaffected skin |
| **Associated features** | Nail thickening, Onycholysis, **Geographic tongue** |
| **Complications** | Erythroderma (if >90% BSA), Hypoalbuminaemia (keratin/protein loss), Hypocalcaemia, Oligaemia, Liver/Kidney/Joint involvement |
| **Labs** | Leukocytosis, ↑ESR, ↓Albumin, ↓Zinc, ↓Calcium |
| **HLA** | HLA-B27 associated |

> **⚠️ Clinical Warning:** Von Zumbusch psoriasis is a **medical emergency** requiring hospitalisation. Complications include multi-organ involvement — not just a skin problem.

> **💡 Why do pustules look like crusting?** Pustules are very small (< 1 mm) and rapidly rupture and erode. The clinical appearance is **erythema with dry, crusted scale** — the individual pustules are not visible to the naked eye unless examined closely.

#### Subtype 2: Generalised Pustular Psoriasis in Pregnancy (Impetigo Herpetiformis)

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Timing** | **Last (3rd) trimester** |
| **Association** | Hypocalcaemia is a key trigger |
| **Fetal risk** | **Placental insufficiency → Stillbirth, Neonatal death, Fetal abnormalities** |
| **Classification** | One of the 5 specific dermatoses of pregnancy |

> **💡 The 5 Dermatoses of Pregnancy (mnemonic: PUPPP + A + P):**
> 1. **PUPPP** — Pruritic Urticarial Papules and Plaques of Pregnancy
> 2. **Pemphigoid gestationis** (Herpes gestationis)
> 3. **Intrahepatic Cholestasis of Pregnancy** (pruritus without rash)
> 4. **Atopic Eruption of Pregnancy**
> 5. **Impetigo Herpetiformis** (Pustular Psoriasis of Pregnancy) — sometimes listed as the 5th

#### Subtype 3: Circinate / Annular / Linear Exanthematous Psoriasis

- Pustules arranged in **ring, circular, or linear patterns**
- Pustules appear **peripherally on the advancing edge** of an expanding erythematous ring
- As pustules dry, they leave a **trailing fringe of scales**

**Key Differential Diagnoses:**

| Differential | Distinguishing Feature |
|---|---|
| **Linear IgA Bullous Dermatosis** | Tense bullae (not pustules); "String of pearls" sign; IgA on BMZ |
| **IgA Pemphigus** | Pustules but in annular pattern; IgA intercellular on biopsy |
| **Sneddon-Wilkinson (Subcorneal Pustular Dermatosis)** | Subcorneal pustules at flexures; distinct biopsy appearance |

#### Subtype 4: Exanthematous Pustular Psoriasis

- Acute, **self-limited** eruption
- Triggered by: **Infection** or **Lithium**
- **No systemic manifestations**
- Most important differential: **AGEP (Acute Generalised Exanthematous Pustulosis)**

| Feature | Exanthematous Psoriasis | AGEP |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | Infection / Lithium | **Ampicillin** (drug reaction) |
| Starting site | Flexures | **Face + Flexures** |
| Onset after trigger | Variable | **Within 24 hours** of drug |
| Biopsy | Neutrophils | **Eosinophils** (drug reaction marker) |

#### Subtype 5: Infantile Generalised Pustular Psoriasis

- **Benign, self-limited**
- No systemic involvement
- Spontaneous remission
- Age: mainly **2–10 years**

#### Subtype 6: Localised (Not on Hands/Feet)

- Pustules appear on the **edge of an existing psoriatic plaque**
- This is a **bad prognostic sign** — indicates the psoriasis is becoming **Unstable**
- May progress to Acute Generalised Pustular Psoriasis or Erythrodermic Psoriasis

## 3. Localised Pustular Psoriasis — Palmoplantar & Acrodermatitis

### Summary

Two types of **Localised Pustular Psoriasis** — both confined to specific anatomical sites:

#### Type 1: Palmoplantar Pustulosis (PPP)

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Distribution** | Bilateral symmetrical sterile pustules on **palms and soles** |
| **Hallmark feature** | **Yellow-brown macules/papules** (as pustules resolve they leave brownish staining — highly characteristic) |
| **Course** | Chronic; **resists treatment** |
| **Associated syndrome** | **SAPHO Syndrome** |
| **Triggering factors** | Focal infection, Smoking, Stress, Lithium |

**SAPHO Syndrome:**
- **S** = Synovitis
- **A** = Acne (severe — Acne conglobata / fulminans)
- **P** = Palmoplantar Pustulosis
- **H** = Hyperostosis
- **O** = Osteitis
> Associated with **Isotretinoin** use because of the Acne component

**Differential Diagnosis of PPP:**
- **Pompholyx (Dyshidrotic Eczema):** Deep-seated vesicles, intensely pruritic, no yellowish-brown staining
- **Vesiculobullous Tinea (Tinea Manuum):** Must specify the vesiculobullous subtype — standard tinea presents as a circinate lesion with active edge
- **Secondary infected eczema:** Pustules are Secondary lesions (from superinfection) — not Primary

> **💡 Primary vs Secondary Pustule Pearl:**
> - **Palmoplantar Pustulosis, Sneddon-Wilkinson, AGEP, Linear IgA** → Pustules are **PRIMARY** lesions (neutrophil-driven disease itself)
> - **Infected Eczema, Infected Herpetic Whitlow** → Pustules are **SECONDARY** (superimposed infection on a pre-existing lesion)

#### Type 2: Acrodermatitis Continua of Hallopeau

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Distribution** | Pustules on the **distal fingertips / toes** (around/under nails) |
| **Nail involvement** | Nail dystrophy, **Nail shedding (Onycholysis/Nail plate loss)** |
| **Bone involvement** | **Osteolysis of distal phalanges** (can destroy bone!) |
| **Associated feature** | **Geographic tongue** (also seen in Von Zumbusch) |
| **Course** | Progressive, destructive |

**Differential Diagnosis of Acrodermatitis Continua:**
- **Herpetic Whitlow** (Herpes Simplex of the finger — vesicles, not pustules primarily)
- **Staphylococcal Paronychia** (bacterial nail fold infection)
- **Contact Dermatitis** of the fingers

## 4. Nail Psoriasis — Features, PDRL & HOOS Mnemonics

### Summary

Nail involvement is extremely important in Psoriasis — it acts as a **marker for Psoriatic Arthritis.**

#### Epidemiology
- **5%** of Psoriasis patients have isolated nail psoriasis (no skin disease)
- **40–45%** of skin Psoriasis patients have nail involvement
- **80–90%** of Psoriatic Arthritis patients have nail involvement → **Always examine the nails when you suspect Psoriatic Arthritis**
- Fingernails > Toenails

#### Anatomy-Based Classification

The nail is affected in two anatomical regions:

| Region | Clinical Features | Mnemonic |
|---|---|---|
| **Nail Matrix** (makes the nail plate) | Pitting, Leukonychia, Red spots in lunula, Nail Plate Dystrophy, Beau's lines | **PDRL** (P=Pitting, D=Dystrophy, R=Red spot, L=Leukonychia) |
| **Nail Bed** (beneath the nail plate) | Hyperkeratosis (subungual), Onycholysis, Oil drop sign (Salmon patch), Splinter haemorrhages | **HOOS** (H=Hyperkeratosis, O=Onycholysis, O=Oil drop, S=Splinter haemorrhage) |

#### Nail Matrix Features (PDRL)

| Sign | Description |
|---|---|
| **Pitting** | Small ice-pick-like pits on nail surface — **most characteristic sign** |
| **Dystrophy (Plate Dystrophy)** | Irregular, crumbling nail plate — end-stage = Trachyonychia (roughened, sandpaper nail) |
| **Red spots in lunula** | Erythematous macules in the white lunula area |
| **Leukonychia** | White patches/spots on the nail plate |
| **Beau's lines** | Transverse grooves across the nail plate |

> **💡 Trachyonychia (20-Nail Dystrophy) — Most Specific For:**
> - **Lichen Planus** (most specific association)
> - **Alopecia Areata** (second most common association)
> - Also seen in Psoriasis

#### Nail Bed Features (HOOS)

| Sign | Description |
|---|---|
| **Hyperkeratosis** | Thickening of the nail bed (subungual hyperkeratosis) |
| **Onycholysis** | Separation of nail plate from nail bed — nail "lifts off" |
| **Oil drop sign (Salmon patch)** | Yellowish-brown discolouration under the nail — **highly specific for Psoriasis** |
| **Splinter haemorrhages** | Linear red-brown streaks under the nail — best seen on Dermatoscopy |

> **💡 Memory Trick:**
> - **PDRL** = like the investigation "VDRL" (for Syphilis) but for the **Nail Matrix**
> - **HOOS** = the Nail **Bed** features (think "Who's in the bed?")

## 5. Psoriatic Arthritis & Systemic Associations

### Summary

#### Psoriatic Arthritis (PsA)

- Occurs in **5–30%** of psoriatic patients
- **Peak age:** 40–60 years
- **Seronegative** arthritis (Rheumatoid Factor negative — differentiates from Rheumatoid Arthritis)
- **HLA-B27** associated

**Temporal relationship with skin disease:**
- Skin disease first → PsA follows: **70% of cases**
- PsA **precedes** skin disease: **15% of cases**
- Simultaneous onset: **15% of cases**

**Five patterns of joint involvement:**

| Pattern | Description |
|---|---|
| **Oligoarthritis (most common ~70%)** | Asymmetric, < 5 joints, often large joints |
| **DIP joint arthritis** | Distal interphalangeal joints → **more specific to PsA** (vs RA which spares DIP) |
| **Symmetric polyarthritis** | Resembles Rheumatoid Arthritis but seronegative |
| **Arthritis mutilans** | Severe destructive: **Osteolysis → Telescoping of digits** ("Opera glass" deformity) |
| **Spondylitis / Sacroiliitis** | Axial spine involvement; associated with HLA-B27 |

**Specific features of PsA (HIGH YIELD):**

| Feature | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Dactylitis ("Sausage digit")** | Diffuse swelling of an entire finger/toe — the whole digit looks like a sausage |
| **Enthesitis** | Inflammation at the **enthesis** = the insertion point of tendon or ligament into bone (e.g., Achilles tendon insertion, plantar fascia) |

#### Systemic Associations of Psoriasis

Psoriasis is a **systemic inflammatory disease.** All physicians managing psoriasis patients must screen for:

| System | Association |
|---|---|
| **Metabolic Syndrome** | Obesity, Hypertension, Dyslipidaemia, Diabetes mellitus |
| **Cardiovascular** | Myocardial infarction, Stroke, Atherosclerosis |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Crohn's Disease, Ulcerative Colitis (Inflammatory Bowel Disease) |
| **Musculoskeletal** | Psoriatic Arthritis |
| **Rheumatological** | SAPHO Syndrome |
| **Psychological** | Depression, Anxiety — significantly impaired Quality of Life |

> **💡 Key Practice Point:** When managing a Psoriasis patient, always ask about: joint pain (PsA), cardiovascular risk factors, weight/BMI, blood sugar, lipids, and mental health. This is what separates a good dermatologist from an average one.

## 6. Severity Assessment — BSA, PASI Score & NAPSI Score

### Summary

Three tools are used to assess disease severity in Psoriasis:

#### Tool 1: Body Surface Area (BSA)

The simplest and most commonly used in clinical practice:

| BSA | Severity |
|---|---|
| **< 3%** | Mild |
| **3–10%** | Moderate |
| **> 10%** | Severe |

- Uses the **Rule of Nines** as a reference for body regions
- **Practical clinical rule:** < 10% BSA → Topical therapy only; > 10% BSA → consider Systemic therapy

#### Tool 2: PASI Score (Psoriasis Area and Severity Index)

The gold-standard research tool. **Range: 0–72.**

**Step 1 — Divide the body into 4 regions with fixed weights:**

| Body Region | Weight (multiplier) |
|---|---|
| Head and Neck | **0.1** |
| Upper Limbs | **0.2** |
| Trunk | **0.3** |
| Lower Limbs | **0.4** |

**Step 2 — For EACH region, score Severity (3 components, each 0–4):**

| Component | Score 0–4 |
|---|---|
| **Erythema** | 0 = none → 4 = most severe |
| **Induration (thickness)** | 0 = none → 4 = most severe |
| **Desquamation (scaling)** | 0 = none → 4 = most severe |

- **Total Severity Score per region = E + I + D (maximum 12)**

**Step 3 — For EACH region, score Area (0–6):**

| Area Score | % of that region affected |
|---|---|
| 0 | None |
| 1 | < 10% |
| 2 | 10–30% |
| 3 | 30–50% |
| 4 | 50–70% |
| 5 | 70–90% |
| 6 | 90–100% |

**Step 4 — Calculate the PASI for each region:**

> **Region PASI = Severity Score × Area Score × Region Weight**

**Step 5 — Add all 4 regions:**

> **Total PASI = Sum of all 4 Region PASI scores (range: 0–72)**

**Interpretation in clinical trials:**
- PASI 75 = 75% reduction in PASI score = standard response threshold for biologic approvals
- PASI 90, PASI 100 = higher response bars used in modern biologic trials

#### Tool 3: NAPSI Score (Nail Psoriasis Severity Index)

For nail involvement specifically. **Range: 0–80 (fingers only) or 0–160 (fingers + toes).**

**Method:**
- Divide each nail into **4 quadrants** with imaginary horizontal and vertical lines
- In each quadrant, score separately for **Nail Matrix** and **Nail Bed**:
  - Score = 0: No features present
  - Score = 1: Matrix features present (PDRL signs)
  - Score = 2: Nail Bed features present (HOOS signs)
  - Score = 2: Both Matrix and Bed features present
- **Maximum per nail = 8** (2 per quadrant × 4 quadrants)
- **Maximum for all 10 fingernails = 80**
- **Maximum for all 20 nails (fingers + toes) = 160**

> **💡 DLQI (Dermatology Life Quality Index):** A third scoring tool assessing the **impact of disease on quality of life** (10 questions, score 0–30). Not a severity score — measures patient-reported impact.

## 7. Topical Treatment of Psoriasis

### Summary

Topical therapies are the **first-line treatment for mild-to-moderate psoriasis** and are always used alongside systemic therapies for severe disease.

**General Measures First:**
- Identify and eliminate environmental triggers (see TIEPOD-LIBA, Lecture 3)
- Emollients (moisturisers) — always used as the base of treatment

#### Drug 1: Topical Corticosteroids — First Line

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Indication** | **First-line** for mild AND moderate psoriasis as monotherapy; adjunct in severe disease |
| **Mechanism** | Anti-inflammatory + Anti-proliferative + Immunosuppressive (non-specific — suppresses DNA of all cells) |
| **Pregnancy category** | **Category C** — can be used, with caution |
| **Potency choice** | Higher potency for thick plaques on trunk/limbs; lower potency (or calcineurin inhibitors) for face, flexures, genitalia |

**Side Effects of Topical Steroids:**

| Side Effect | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Skin atrophy** | Thinning of skin with prolonged use |
| **Telangiectasia** | Permanent dilated vessels |
| **Striae** | Stretch marks — irreversible |
| **Perioral/Periocular Rosacea** | Especially with fluorinated steroids on the face |
| **Glaucoma / Cataract** | With periorbital use |
| **Tachyphylaxis** | Loss of efficacy with prolonged continuous use — the skin "gets used to" the steroid and stops responding |

> **⚠️ Contraindications:** Active bacterial or viral infections at the application site

#### Drug 2: Topical Calcineurin Inhibitors (Tacrolimus, Pimecrolimus)

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Role** | **Steroid-sparing** agents — used where steroids are risky |
| **Best sites** | Face, eyelids, intertriginous areas (axillae, groin), genitalia |
| **Mechanism** | Blocks Calcineurin → prevents NFAT dephosphorylation → no T-cell activation → no IL-2 → no T-cell proliferation |
| **Specificity** | Works on **any T-cell** (helper and cytotoxic) — not organ-specific |
| **Systemic equivalent** | Cyclosporine (same Calcineurin pathway) |
| **Side effects** | **Burning and itching sensation** (tip: advise patient to keep it in the fridge) |
| **Malignancy** | Animal studies showed risk; **not proven in humans** |
| **Pregnancy** | Category C |

#### Drug 3: Vitamin D3 Analogues (Calcipotriol / Calcitriol)

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Examples** | Calcipotriol 50 mcg/g (Daivonex), Calcipotriol + Betamethasone (Daivobet) |
| **Mechanism** | Reduces keratinocyte proliferation + Normalises keratinocyte differentiation |
| **Maximum dose** | **100 g per week** — must not exceed (risk of hypercalcaemia) |
| **Side effects** | Local irritation; **Reversible hypercalcaemia** (with overuse) |
| **Effect on PTH** | Decreases parathyroid hormone |
| **Contraindications** | Large surface area, abnormal bone metabolism, hypercalcaemia, **renal insufficiency, pregnancy, lactation** |

> **💡 Combination:** Calcipotriol + Betamethasone (Daivobet) is a very commonly prescribed combination — the steroid reduces inflammation rapidly, the vitamin D analogue maintains long-term normalisation of proliferation.

#### Drug 4: Topical Retinoids (Tazarotene)

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Concentration** | 0.05% and 0.1% (cream or gel) |
| **Maximum treatment area** | 10–20% of body surface area |
| **Mechanism** | Decreases keratinocyte hyperproliferation + Normalises differentiation + Decreases inflammatory cytokine expression |
| **Side effects** | Local irritation (lesional and perilesional skin), **Photosensitivity** |
| **Pregnancy** | **Category X — CONTRAINDICATED (teratogenic)** — applies to topical AND systemic retinoids |

## 8. Overview — Full Psoriasis Treatment Ladder

### Summary

*(Note: The audio transcript introduced this overview at the end of the lecture; full detail on Systemic and Phototherapy will be covered in Lecture 5.)*

The complete treatment ladder for Psoriasis:

| Level | Modality | Agents |
|---|---|---|
| **1st** | Topical | Steroids, Calcineurin inhibitors, Vitamin D3, Retinoids, Coal tar, Anthralin, Salicylic acid, Emollients |
| **2nd** | Phototherapy | NB-UVB (narrowband UVB 311–313 nm), PUVA (Psoralen + UVA), Excimer laser |
| **3rd** | Systemic conventional | Methotrexate (MTX), Cyclosporine, Acitretin |
| **4th** | Biologics | Anti-TNF-α (Infliximab, Adalimumab, Etanercept), Anti-IL17 (Secukinumab, Ixekizumab, Brodalumab), Anti-IL12/23 (Ustekinumab), Anti-IL23 p19 (Guselkumab, Risankizumab) |
| **Special** | Photodynamic therapy, Laser (Pulsed dye, CO2, Excimer) | Site-specific or adjunctive |

**Special situations requiring specific systemic choices:**
- **Pustular Psoriasis** → Acitretin (drug of choice) + supportive care
- **Erythrodermic Psoriasis** → Hospitalisation; Cyclosporine or Methotrexate
- **Psoriasis in Pregnancy** → Topical steroids preferred; NB-UVB; avoid systemics where possible; Cyclosporine if severe
- **Psoriasis + Liver disease** → UVB + Etanercept (avoid Methotrexate — hepatotoxic)

## 9. MCQ Practice & High-Yield Summary

### Summary

#### Key High-Yield Facts for Examination

**Pustular Psoriasis:**
- Von Zumbusch = **Emergency** = Generalised + systemic features
- Pregnancy type = **Last trimester** + fetal risk + hypocalcaemia trigger
- AGEP differential = **Ampicillin** + eosinophils on biopsy (not neutrophils)
- Infantile = **Benign, self-limited**
- Hallopeau = **Distal phalanx osteolysis** + geographic tongue

**Nail Psoriasis:**
- 80–90% of Psoriatic Arthritis patients have nail involvement
- Matrix features = **PDRL** (Pitting, Dystrophy, Red spot, Leukonychia)
- Nail bed features = **HOOS** (Hyperkeratosis, Onycholysis, Oil drop, Splinter haemorrhage)
- **Trachyonychia** most specific for Lichen Planus (then Alopecia Areata)
- **Oil drop / Salmon patch** = most specific nail sign for Psoriasis

**Psoriatic Arthritis:**
- **DIP** joints > PIP joints (opposite of RA)
- **Dactylitis** = Sausage digit (whole digit swollen)
- **Enthesitis** = tendon/ligament insertion inflammation
- **Arthritis Mutilans** = telescoping digits (worst form)
- HLA-B27 = Spondylitis + Pustular Psoriasis
- PsA **precedes** skin disease in **15%** of cases

**Severity Assessment:**
- BSA: Mild < 3%, Moderate 3–10%, Severe > 10%
- PASI = 0–72; formula = Severity × Area × Region weight, summed for 4 regions
- NAPSI = 0–80 (fingers) or 0–160 (all nails)
- **PASI 75** = standard biologic response threshold

**Topical Treatments:**
- **First line** = Topical Corticosteroids
- **Steroid-sparing** = Tacrolimus/Pimecrolimus (face, flexures)
- **Calcipotriol max dose** = **100 g/week** (hypercalcaemia risk)
- **Retinoids** = Category **X** (all — topical and systemic)
- **Goeckerman** = Coal tar + UV
- **Ingram** = Anthralin + UV
- **Tachyphylaxis** = loss of steroid efficacy with prolonged use
- **Anthralin** = Short contact therapy, brown staining side effect

#### MCQ Practice from Lecture

**Q1:** MHC Class I molecules — which T-cell do they present antigen to?
**A:** CD8+ (T-Cytotoxic) — MHC I × CD8 = 8; MHC II × CD4 = 8

**Q2:** Psoriatic Arthritis is most commonly associated with which HLA?
**A:** **HLA-B27** (especially spondylitis + pustular type)

**Q3:** Which antibody binds the Fc-epsilon receptor (FcεRI) on Mast cells?
**A:** **IgE** — the basis of Type I (immediate) hypersensitivity and Urticaria

**Q4:** 35-year-old woman with tender nodules on shins, dry cough, red eye history → Biopsy: septal panniculitis → Diagnosis?
**A:** **Sarcoidosis** — presenting as **Löfgren Syndrome** (triad: Erythema Nodosum + Ankle arthritis/arthralgia + Hilar lymphadenopathy)
- Note: TB also causes Erythema Nodosum, but the eye + chest + joint triad → Sarcoidosis
- Histopathology: Septal panniculitis (vacuolation of fat cells in septa)

**Q5:** Intensely pruritic upper-back lesion in Indian woman for 6 years, biopsy shows non-specific features → Stain needed?
**A:** **Crystal violet** — stain for **Macular Amyloidosis**
